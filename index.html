War Room: Listening & Film Analysis

English Through Films • Individual Worksheet

Learning focus: Understand spoken dialogue without subtitles; interpret expressions and speaker intentions; observe nonverbal communication; and explain how dialogue and nonverbal cues contribute to a scene's meaning and tone.

How to use this worksheet: Open the full film War Room. The scene descriptions and dialogue cues below will help you locate relevant moments; exact timestamps are not required. For Part A, turn subtitles off before listening. Replay a scene when needed. For Parts B and C, watch the characters as well as listening to them.

Part A • Listening comprehension

Listen to the Dialogue

For each item, locate the scene using the description, listen with subtitles OFF, and select the best answer.

Scene 1 — The family dinner argumentLook for the conversation after Tony notices that money has been transferred from the couple's savings account to Elizabeth's checking account.
1. What is the main disagreement between Tony and Elizabeth in this conversation?
2. When Tony says that he makes “four times what you do,” what is he implying?
Scene 2 — Tony and Michael after basketballFind the scene in which Michael notices Tony's behavior during the basketball game and later challenges him about his marriage.
3. What does Michael mean when he says, “I'd like to see the church in you, bro”?
4. Why does Michael compare Tony's marriage to a person who is bleeding while he continues eating his salad?
Scene 3 — Clara introduces Elizabeth to the War RoomFind the conversation in Clara's house in which she asks Elizabeth about her prayer life and shows her the closet.
5. When Clara says that people may drink coffee hot or cold but nobody likes it “lukewarm,” what is she comparing to lukewarm coffee?
6. What does Clara mean when she tells Elizabeth that she has been “fighting the wrong enemy”?
Scene 4 — The robbery and its aftermathFind the scene in which a robber threatens Elizabeth and Clara with a knife, followed by the conversation with the police.
7. Why does Clara tell the robber to put the knife down “in the name of Jesus”?
Scene 5 — Elizabeth prays alone in the War RoomLocate the scene after Elizabeth receives a message suggesting Tony has been seen with another woman.
8. What is Elizabeth asking for when she says, “I'm not his judge, you are”?
Scene 6 — Tony admits his wrongdoingFind the moment when Tony speaks privately to Jesus and acknowledges his selfishness, pride, and the harm he has caused his family.
9. What does Tony's statement, “I don't like the man I've become,” reveal?
Scene 7 — Tony returns the samples to his former employerFind the meeting in which Tony brings back the company's samples, confesses what he did, and accepts the possible consequences.
10. What does Tony demonstrate when he says he is ready to accept “whatever the consequence”?
11. Coleman decides not to prosecute Tony, although Tony must repay the money. What does this response most clearly illustrate?
Scene 8 — Tony explains his new job decision to ElizabethFind the later conversation in which Tony tells Elizabeth about the community-center job and she responds that she would rather have a man chasing Jesus than a house full of things.
12. What does Elizabeth mean by “I would rather have a man chasing Jesus than a house full of stuff”?
Part B • Nonverbal communication

Watch the Characters

Rewatch each scene with the picture visible. Pay attention to facial expressions, eye contact, posture, gestures, physical distance, movement, pauses, and vocal delivery. Describe only cues you actually observe in the film.

Scene 1 — The family dinner argumentObserve Tony, Elizabeth, and Danielle during and immediately after the disagreement.
13. Identify one nonverbal cue from Tony or Elizabeth. What emotion or attitude does it communicate, and how does it affect the tension of the scene?
Scene 2 — Tony and Michael after basketballObserve Tony's movements and interaction with Michael before their conversation about Tony's marriage.
14. What do Tony's physical behavior and vocal delivery suggest about his emotional state? Support your answer with one specific cue.
Scene 3 — Clara confronts the robberCompare Clara's response with Elizabeth's reaction when the robber threatens them.
15. How do the two women's voices, facial expressions, posture, or actions differ in this moment? What does the contrast communicate about their responses to danger?
Scene 4 — Elizabeth in the War RoomObserve Elizabeth during her private prayer after receiving the message about Tony.
16. Identify one change in Elizabeth's expression, posture, gestures, or vocal delivery during this prayer. How does the change communicate her emotional state?
Scene 5 — Tony confesses to ColemanObserve Tony as he returns the samples and accepts responsibility, then compare his delivery with the earlier scenes in which he argues with Elizabeth.
17. What is different about Tony's nonverbal behavior or vocal delivery during the confession? How does it help communicate his change as a character?
Part C • Final reflection

Connecting Dialogue, Behavior, Meaning, and Tone

18. Choose one scene from War Room. Explain how a specific line of dialogue and at least one nonverbal cue work together to communicate the character's feelings or intentions. How do they shape the tone of the scene?
19. Based on the film, what message does War Room communicate about conflict, forgiveness, responsibility, or faith? Support your interpretation with one specific scene or moment from the film.
